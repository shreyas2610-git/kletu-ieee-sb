IEEE KLETU Student Branch · About

Fifteen years in the making

The IEEE Student Branch at KLE Technological University began with a small cohort and a conviction that great engineers are shaped by community as much as coursework. This is that story.

2009
2009

The founding

The IEEE Student Branch at KLE Technological University was established in 2009 by a group of faculty and students who recognized the gap between what a classroom teaches and what an engineer needs. With the endorsement of the IEEE Bangalore Section and an initial cohort of fewer than 30 members, the branch held its first event — a microcontroller programming workshop — in a borrowed seminar hall.

Resources were minimal. What the founding members had in abundance was conviction: that exposure to a global professional network, access to research literature, and participation in technical competitions would compound into something significant over a career. That conviction has guided every decision since.

2012 – 2016

Growth and first chapters

By 2012 the branch had grown steadily and launched its first formal society chapter, giving students in computing and electronics a dedicated technical community within IEEE. Annual events began to take on names and reputations — inter-college paper presentation contests, industry visits to campuses in Pune and Bengaluru, and the branch's first participation in IEEEXtreme.

A mentorship program pairing incoming students with experienced IEEE members started in this period and became one of the branch's most durable traditions. Senior members share not just technical knowledge but the habits of mind that make an engineer effective: curiosity, precision, and the willingness to work through problems that don't have clean answers.

The branch's best asset has always been its seniors — students who stayed and taught the ones who came after them.
2019 – 2021

Staying together remotely

The pandemic closed campuses in early 2020. The branch response was straightforward: pivot online, and keep going. More than ten webinars ran in 2020, including sessions with IEEE Distinguished Speakers joining from the US, Singapore, and the UK. Membership actually grew during this period — students who had less access to campus found that a global professional network was, if anything, more valuable when physical proximity was impossible.

The experience established digital event production as a branch competency, one that has been applied to hybrid formats in subsequent years to expand reach beyond the KLE Tech campus.

2022 – present

Today

The branch now operates five chapters and affinity group petitions — Computer Society (approved April 2026), Power Electronics Society, Robotics and Automation Society, Women in Engineering, and ComSoc — covering the main technical and professional communities within IEEE. Membership has grown to 150+ active students in a single academic year.

Recognition at Avinya Shishir 2025, IEEE Xplore publications from branch members, and a record 20+ events in 2023–24 mark the branch's most productive stretch. The work is incremental and deliberate: build things worth doing, document them honestly, and leave the branch in better shape than you found it.
